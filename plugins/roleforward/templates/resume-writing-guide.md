# Resume Writing Guide

The rubric for drafting, assembling, and auditing <Name>'s resume. The research-backed base below applies to any field. <Name>'s own style preferences go in House Rules, and the variant recipes are written once the portfolio has tagged lines.

## House Rules

- **One accurate resume, no inflation:** every claim must survive an interview probe. Program-level or team results are framed as scope ("led the team that delivered"), never as personal output.
- **Verified numbers only:** pull metrics from the portfolio, which tags each for source and scope. If a number isn't confirmed, leave it out rather than guess. Never invent or estimate a number to fill the formula below: concrete evidence without a number is better than a number nobody can back up.
- **Disclosure rules:** follow the portfolio's Disclosure Rules in every output.
- **No sensitive content:** no comp, performance ratings, or confidential details.

## Content and Impact

- **Use the XYZ formula on every bullet:** "Accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z" (Laszlo Bock, Google). Lead with the outcome, prove it, then name the action.
- **Proof is a number when there's an honest one,** and concrete evidence when there isn't: the scale it runs at (requests a second, users, data volume), who adopted or relied on it, what shipped and where, what it replaced, or who chose it over alternatives. Much engineering, design, and early-career work has no clean metric, and specific evidence reads as well as a number. "Built the payment retry service that every checkout flow now uses" beats an estimated percentage.
- **Quantify where you honestly can, and drop "responsible for":** replace duty language with achievement verbs (designed, led, reduced, built, delivered).
- **Front-load:** put the strongest, most-quantified bullet first in each role. Recruiters skim from the top in seconds.
- **Show skills in context:** a skill named without a supporting accomplishment reads thin, and modern screeners compare the whole resume to the job description.
- **Length:** one page early in a career, and two pages is normal for senior roles. Never pad.

## Passing Applicant Tracking Systems and AI Screening

- **Layout:** single column. Tables, text boxes, columns, and graphics lose content to parsers.
- **Headers:** standard ones (Summary, Experience, Skills, Education). Projects, Publications, and similar sections are fine when they carry real evidence, and many technical resumes leave out the Summary.
- **File type:** a text-based PDF or .docx, never an image or scan.
- **Mirror the posting's language:** match the job description's exact terms where they're true. Put the target title in the headline, and reuse key terms in the competencies and inside bullets.
- **No keyword stuffing:** every keyword should tie to a bullet that shows it in action.
- **Match rate:** check the match against each specific posting before sending (Jobscan cites about 75% as a good target).

## Building Variants

A variant is one headline, one summary, an ordered competency block, and a selected, ordered set of portfolio resume lines. Choose along two axes:
- **Track lean:** leadership, technical or individual contributor, or balanced.
- **Focus tilt:** which area the target role centers.

Select lines by their tags: the track tag picks the lean, the area tag picks the tilt, and the tier tag decides default inclusion. Mirror the target title in the headline, reorder the competencies to match, and stay within the page limit.

## Audit Rubric (score Y or N before calling a resume final)

1. Every bullet uses XYZ (outcome, a metric or concrete evidence, action), not duty language.
2. The strongest bullet (the biggest result, best proven) leads each role.
3. Every metric traces to a portfolio resume line with the right scope.
4. No team or program number is framed as personal output.
5. Single column, with no tables, text boxes, or graphics.
6. Standard section headers.
7. The target title is in the headline, matched to the posting.
8. Keywords are mirrored from the posting, and each one is tied to a bullet.
9. The right variant is selected for the target.
10. It's within the page limit, with no padding.
11. It follows <Name>'s style rules.
12. No sensitive content, and the disclosure rules are followed.
13. Submitted as a text-based PDF or .docx.
